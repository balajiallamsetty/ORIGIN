Editorial standards

Publication Ethics

ORIGIN expects authors, reviewers, and editors to uphold research integrity throughout the publication process.

Policy

Responsibilities of the research community

ORIGIN aligns its editorial practice with widely recognized guidance from the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE). This statement does not constitute a claim of official COPE membership.

Research Integrity

Authentic and responsibly conducted research is the foundation of scholarly publishing. Authors should report methods and findings with enough transparency for independent evaluation, and should not misrepresent the origin, ownership, or contribution of the work.

Plagiarism

ORIGIN maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward plagiarism, including unattributed copying of text, figures, data, or ideas. Manuscripts found to contain plagiarized material are subject to rejection or, if already published, to correction or retraction.

Data Fabrication & Falsification

Research data must be authentic and accurately represented. Fabricating results, manipulating images or datasets in a misleading way, or omitting inconvenient observations is a serious breach of publication ethics.

Duplicate / Redundant Publication

Manuscripts should not be simultaneously submitted to more than one journal, and should not republish substantially the same findings without transparent disclosure and editorial agreement.

Peer Review Integrity

Reviewers and editors must treat manuscripts as confidential, assess them impartially, and recuse themselves when they cannot provide an unbiased evaluation. Authors must not attempt to influence review through undisclosed relationships or identity cues in blinded submissions.

Conflicts of Interest

Authors, reviewers, and editors should disclose financial, personal, or professional relationships that could reasonably be perceived to influence the work, the review, or the editorial decision.

Corrections & Retractions

Published records may require corrections or retractions when significant errors or integrity issues are identified. ORIGIN will act to preserve the accuracy of the scholarly record rather than to protect reputation at the expense of transparency.

Detailed journal-specific policies and procedures may be expanded as ORIGIN's publication program develops.
